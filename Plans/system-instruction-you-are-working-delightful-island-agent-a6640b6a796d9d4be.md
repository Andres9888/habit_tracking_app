## 1. Core scientific claims

Suzuki frames aerobic exercise as the most powerful, evidence-backed intervention for immediate and long-term brain function. Her consistent claims across talks and her book *Healthy Brain, Happy Life* (Dey Street, 2015):

- **Hippocampus & prefrontal cortex are the two brain regions most susceptible to neurodegeneration** and **most responsive to aerobic exercise**. A single workout increases neurotransmitter levels of **dopamine, serotonin, and noradrenaline**, which is why you feel better right after.
- **BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)** — she calls it "Miracle-Gro for the brain." Cardio elevates BDNF, which supports the birth of new hippocampal neurons (adult neurogenesis) and strengthens synaptic plasticity.
- **Long-term exercise physically enlarges the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex** — she frequently cites this as creating a "bigger, stronger" hippocampus and PFC that are "more resistant to neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's and dementia."
- **Immediate mood lift** from a single session; **sustained structural change** from regular training.

## 2. Exercise prescription (her stated dose)

Suzuki's most-repeated prescription in interviews (Huberman, Attia, Rich Roll, TED):

- **Frequency:** "**3–4 times a week**" minimum; **"more is better"** up to daily.
- **Duration:** **"30–45 minutes"** per session of aerobic work (she sometimes phrases it as "at least 30 minutes").
- **Intensity:** Enough to **"get your heart rate up"** — moderate to vigorous. She often cites **aiming to break a sweat** and being able to talk but not sing as a rule of thumb.
- **Modality:** Anything aerobic — **walking (brisk), jogging, cycling, dancing, swimming, intenSati** (a cardio + affirmation class she co-created and teaches).
- **Key framing:** **"Even a 10-minute walk"** produces measurable mood and focus improvements — she uses this as the low-floor entry point, then encourages building to the 30–45 min / 3–4x standard.

## 3. Cognitive / mood benefits cited

- **Attention and prefrontal function:** Improved sustained attention and executive function after acute bouts; measurable in her NYU lab's Stroop and reaction-time tasks.
- **Working memory & long-term memory:** Hippocampal-dependent memory improves; she cites being able to "remember more and remember for longer."
- **Mood:** Decreased anxiety and depressive symptoms; she describes cardio as delivering a "bubble bath of neurochemicals."
- **Dementia protection:** Her headline claim — exercise is **"the single most transformative thing you can do for your brain today"** because it **"can protect your brain from the incurable diseases like Alzheimer's disease and dementia."** She does not typically quote specific effect sizes on stage; she references the broader epidemiological literature (e.g., Erickson, Kramer) showing **~2% hippocampal volume increase** over a year of aerobic training in older adults — though that specific number is from Erickson et al. 2011, which she cites.

## 6. Peer-reviewed citation

**Suzuki lab / directly supporting the cardio-brain claim:**

- Basso, J. C., & **Suzuki, W. A.** (2017). *The Effects of Acute Exercise on Mood, Cognition, Neurophysiology, and Neurochemical Pathways: A Review.* **Brain Plasticity, 2(2), 127–152.**
  DOI: `10.3233/BPL-160040`
  Publisher URL: `https://content.iospress.com/articles/brain-plasticity/bpl160040`
  This is her lab's canonical review tying a single bout of aerobic exercise to measurable mood, cognition, BDNF, and catecholamine changes — the direct scientific backing for her popular talks.

Supporting citation she frequently references (not her lab, but foundational and cited in her talks):
- Erickson, K. I., et al. (2011). *Exercise training increases size of hippocampus and improves memory.* **PNAS 108(7), 3017–3022.** DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1015950108`.
