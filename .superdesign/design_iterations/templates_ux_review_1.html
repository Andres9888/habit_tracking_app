Templates Screen UX Review

Side-by-side comparison of current implementation vs proposed improvements, with reasoning for each change.

1
Remove Redundant Preview Button
Must Fix

The entire card is already tappable for preview (line 87: onPress={() => onPreview(item)}). The separate "Preview" button wastes vertical space, creates decision friction ("which do I tap?"), and pushes the "Add" button further from the template title. Removing it makes the card lighter and the primary action (Add) more prominent.

Why this matters
Fewer buttons = faster decisions. Hick's Law: decision time increases with the number of choices. With one button, users tap "Add" or tap the card. Two buttons force a pause: "wait, which one do I want?" The "Added" state also benefits from a check icon (universally understood) over text, especially at 78px width where "Added" barely fits.
2
Fix Loading State Transition
Must Fix

The loading skeleton shows a different subtitle than the loaded view. The shimmer also lacks the QuickFilterChips placeholder, causing a visible layout shift when data arrives. Both issues break the illusion of instant loading.

Why this matters
Skeleton screens work by anchoring expectations. When the skeleton layout doesn't match the loaded layout, the "seamless" transition becomes a jarring jump. The subtitle change is especially noticeable because text is the first thing users read. Adding a chip shimmer row prevents the content below from shifting down by ~48px when chips appear.
3
Add Scroll Affordance to Filter Chips
Polish

8 chips with showsHorizontalScrollIndicator={false} and symmetric padding. ~5.5 chips fit on a 375pt screen. The last visible chip appears complete, so there's zero visual hint that more categories exist off-screen.

Why this matters
Discoverability drives engagement. Users who never discover "Finance" or "Learning" chips won't use category filtering for those topics. A subtle edge fade is the standard iOS/Android pattern for signaling scrollable overflow (used in Apple's App Store category chips, Spotify's browse pills, etc.). Costs ~5 lines of code.
4
Make Search Match Reason Scannable
Polish

In search results, the match reason is the key contextual information explaining why each result appeared. Currently it's just small bold green text at the bottom of the card — easy to miss in a long list.

Why this matters
Search confidence = conversion. When users search "dopamine" and see "Match: research summary", they understand why the result appeared and trust the search engine. A subtle background tint + search icon makes this information scannable in a list of 10+ results without reading every card's full text. Also note the meta pill icons — emoji prefixes add visual anchors that help eyes jump to the right pill.
5
Increase Icon Background Opacity
Polish

The icon wrapper uses ${iconColor}20 which is ~12.5% opacity. Against the white card background it's barely visible, weakening the visual anchor that helps users scan the list.
