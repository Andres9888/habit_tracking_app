Rationale

Each change, and the problem it solves

# Customer pain point Change Experience improvement Problem solved
1 Silent failure. "I turned reminders on and never got one. I lost a 40-day streak." Today the toggle sits on while the OS permission is off; the only signal is a passive amber strip inside the card, below the fold. A Needs-attention strip at the top of Settings with a one-tap Fix that deep-links to the OS permission screen, plus the toggle itself rendering off (pin 3). The failure is visible before the user scrolls, and recovering takes one tap instead of a hunt through iOS Settings. Breaks the app's single worst trust failure: a control that reports success while doing nothing. Streak loss is the top uninstall trigger in habit apps — the feature the user is paying for was silently dead.
2 "Where do I cancel?" Sign out, email, subscription and delete-account all live behind a card whose only label is a name and "Tap to edit profile". Trial status is a banner five cards down. Hero subtitle names its contents — Plan & billing · Email · Sign out — and carries a live plan chip. Trial countdown and the upgrade CTA move into the hero. Account tasks become findable by reading, not by exploratory tapping. Trial users see days remaining on the first screenful. Kills the highest-volume support ticket class (billing/cancellation) and removes end-of-trial surprise, which drives both refund requests and one-star reviews.
3 Lying controls. Reminder state shows the user's intent, not the system's reality. Toggle derives from enabled && permissionGranted. When blocked it reads off with an inline reason: "Blocked by iOS — not sending". What the screen shows is what the system will do. No second interpretation needed. Restores the core contract of a settings screen: state display equals state. Also removes the amber banner as a permanent tenant of the card.
4 "My tap did nothing." Theme, Sort order and reminder time expand inline, so the page grows under the thumb. The code carries explicit rescue work for this — useEnsureVisible, handlePickerLayout, and a RESERVED_BANNER_HEIGHT spacer to stop the list jumping. Inline expanders become bottom sheets / sub-pages. Rows keep their current value on the right and a chevron; the list height never changes. No content jumps, no picker opening below the fold, no lost scroll position. Choosing feels like the Calendar-look flow the user already knows. Removes a whole class of "unresponsive UI" perception, and retires the scroll-rescue machinery that exists only to compensate for the pattern.
5 Grouping by internals. "Habits" holds three unrelated jobs: ordering, recovery, and data ownership. A user wanting their data out has no reason to open a card called Habits, and one wanting a deleted habit back is in a panic state. Rename to Your habits & data; subtitles restated as outcomes ("Restore a habit you hid", "CSV or JSON — yours to keep"). Support becomes Help & about with feedback promoted to first row. Section names match the words users arrive with, so scanning stops at the right card instead of opening all six. Fixes findability at the label layer. Worth noting: search plumbing (SettingsSearchProvider, sectionHasMatch) is wired through every section but has no entry point — evidence findability was already failing. Better labels are the cheaper fix; the dead search branches should then be deleted.

Deliberately unchanged

  • · Editorial header (eyebrow + 33px serif) — distinctive and working.
  • · Card + colour-tile row anatomy — consistent and legible at 56px targets.
  • · 56px gap isolating delete-account on the Account sub-page — already correct.
  • · Quiet footer with selectable build number for support triage.

Scope note

This is a static critique artifact. No files under src/ were touched. Pins 1 and 3 depend on reading live permission state into the toggle; pin 4 is a container swap, not a logic change; pins 2 and 5 are copy and ordering. Sequenced by customer cost, 1 and 3 ship first — they are the only ones where the current build actively misinforms.
