Why Option A?
1. Compact Header = More Habit Cards
The shelf area is prime real estate. Every extra row pushes habit cards down. Option B adds ~30px of vertical space — that's nearly half a habit card lost below the fold. Users with 5+ habits feel this.
2. Nudge, Not a Shout
The Zeigarnik effect works best as a subtle itch, not a billboard. A small green pill next to the date creates "oh, I'm close" — putting it front-and-center below the strip makes it feel like a progress bar UX that nags.
3. Shared Slot = Clean State Machine
The right side of the WeekNavRow is a single slot that cycles through states: streak pill, "Almost perfect!", "Streak at risk", or "Today →". One slot, one piece of contextual info. No layout shift between states.
4. Information Hierarchy Preserved
Day strip rings = primary (where you act). Badge = secondary context. In Option B, the badge sits between the rings and habit cards, breaking the visual flow from "see progress" → "take action." Option A keeps it metadata-level.
Trade-off to Consider
If the badge replaces the streak pill (which it does — they're mutually exclusive), users lose streak visibility at the exact moment they're closest to a perfect day. The streak context might actually help motivate finishing. This is fine because "almost perfect" is the stronger motivator in that moment.
When Option B Wins
If user research shows people aren't noticing the badge in its current spot and are missing the "one left" moment, then the below-strip placement makes sense. But optimize for that after you have signal — don't add vertical weight preemptively.
