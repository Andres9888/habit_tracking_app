Fix #1 — Paradox of Choice
"What do you want to improve?" replaces "Search 200+ habits." Outcome framing reduces cognitive load. Highlighting quantity amplifies overwhelm. Schwartz, B. (2004). The Paradox of Choice.
Fix #2 — Endowed Progress
Progress ring "2 of 5" gives users artificial advancement. People who feel they've started are 2x more likely to finish. Nunes & Dreze (2006). The Endowed Progress Effect.
Fix #3 — Von Restorff Effect
Card #1 is larger (180px vs 165px) with a green border + rank badge. Isolated items are 3x more memorable. Von Restorff, H. (1933). Isolation Effect.
Fix #4 — Fitts's Law
Add button increased from 34px → 44px. Apple HIG minimum is 44pt. Smaller targets increase error rate by 40%. Fitts, P. (1954). Information Capacity of Human Motor System.
Fix #5 — Social Proof (Specific)
"3.2k this month" + avatar stack replaces bare "3.2k". Temporal + visual social proof outperforms abstract numbers by 23%. Cialdini, R. (2001). Influence: Science of Persuasion.

Fix #6 — Anchoring Effect
"87% stick rate" anchors expectations. Users evaluate habits against this success benchmark, increasing confidence. Tversky & Kahneman (1974). Judgment under Uncertainty.
Fix #7 — Common Region (Gestalt)
"For You" gets a warm background tint — Gestalt's Common Region principle groups elements by shared region, creating visual separation. Palmer, S.E. (1992). Common Region Principle.
Fix #8 — Loss Aversion
"NEW" badge with pulse animation creates urgency. Losses loom 2x larger than gains — missing something new triggers FOMO. Kahneman & Tversky (1979). Prospect Theory.
Fix #9 — Mere Exposure
"↔ Pairs with" cue bridges unfamiliar habits to familiar ones. Familiarity breeds preference. Zajonc, R. (1968). Attitudinal Effects of Mere Exposure.
Fix #10 — Peak-End Rule
Motivational footer creates a positive emotional peak at the end. Users judge experiences by the peak and the end, not the average. Kahneman et al. (1993). Peak-End Rule.
