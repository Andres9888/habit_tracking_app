Why both changes, now

Small, but both are the kind of issues that compound in cost if they aren't fixed before ship. They're also cheap to fix — under five lines of diff between them — so there's no reason to defer.

Semantics have a half-life. Every time the amber dot fires for "new content," users learn that amber = nudge. The first time warning actually means warning — streak at risk, data loss, payment failure — it won't land. Fix it while the user base is still small.
Ship safety. The bypass was a review-pass tool. Anyone who sets an env var gets past auth. Not a theoretical risk — it would ship disabled auth. Revert is non-negotiable before merge.
Palette discipline. Every time a token is used for a meaning it doesn't own, the design system erodes. Swap costs five characters; not swapping costs every future audit.
Zero user-facing cost. No UX regression, no layout change, no copy change. Both are internal hygiene.
