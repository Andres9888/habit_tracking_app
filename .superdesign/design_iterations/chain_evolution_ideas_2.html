CHAIN IMPROVEMENTS · ROUND 2

Six more ways to make the chain do more work

Round 1 shipped material tiers and forge & repair. These six focus on scalability (long chains), emotional resonance (personal archive), and quiet retention nudges.

01

Chain compression at length

Once the chain exceeds ~14 links, the middle collapses into a dotted spine. The 5 most-recent days stay full-detail. Milestone dots (gold / amber) punctuate the history. Swipe left to expand a section.

Why: the current 5-day window only answers "what's next?" At 147 days, users want to see the scope of what they've built — and to spot where they broke. Pure render logic, no schema.

02

Long-press link biography

Long-press any past link → a tooltip card surfaces the context of that day, drawn entirely from data you already store (timestamp, streak position, habit-strength snapshot).

Why: turns the chain from a progress bar into a searchable diary. Zero new backend — every field above is already in your completions table.

03

Anchor moments

Long-press-and-hold any past link → pin it as personally meaningful with a short label. Pinned links show a small star badge and persist forever, even through tier upgrades. Costs one boolean field on completions.

★
Anchored: "First 5K"

Why: users suddenly have reasons to revisit their chain. Milestone + story = sticky.

04

Milestone narrative card

At day 30 / 100 / 365, surface a two-sentence story generated from the user's own data. Template + data (no AI call). Shareable as an image — this is the moment users screenshot and post.

Day 100 · Meditate
The chain is solid gold.

You began on March 1st. You missed twice — once on a snow day, once the week your grandmother passed. You came back both times. Longest run: 63 days.

Copy generation: template chooses based on missed-day count, habit name, and time-of-year tags — e.g., weather API → "snow day," personal notes → "grandmother's funeral." Deterministic, user-controllable, local.

05

Retroactive log — honest visual

Let users fill a missed day after the fact, but render it with diagonal stripes — clearly distinct from a real forge. Fresh forge = solid. Retroactive = striped. Missed = broken. Three states, unambiguous.

Fresh forge
Retroactive
Missed (broken)

Why: "I forgot to tap but I actually did it" is the #1 complaint in habit apps. Let users fix it without lying about history — the stripes keep the chain truthful.

Edge: allow retroactive within 48 h of the missed day only. Beyond that, it stays broken — respecting the line between forgetting and rewriting history.

06

Breaking-point marker

Once the user passes their own median break length (calculated from their historical streak lengths), render a subtle tick mark on the chain. No copy, no moralizing — the chain quietly signals "you're in new territory." Self-referential only; zero social comparison.

Journal
Streak 28 days
DAY 1 Your usual ·
You've passed your usual break point. Day 28 — that's new territory.

Why: the threat isn't external judgment — it's your own pattern. Works because the chain surfaces a fact they already know but haven't articulated: "I always break around here."

Recommended order

1. Long-press biography (02) — zero schema, high emotional payoff.
2. Retroactive log (05) — solves a real pain that will otherwise generate support tickets.
3. Anchor moments (03) — one boolean field; makes the chain a keepsake.
4+. Compression, narrative card, breaking-point — bigger lifts, ship once 1-3 prove the pattern.
