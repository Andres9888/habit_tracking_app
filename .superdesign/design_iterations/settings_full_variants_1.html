Recommendation: Variant 2 — Apple Settings
The short version: Variant 2 wins because it scores highest on familiarity, scannability, and implementation feasibility while delivering real UX and monetization improvements.

Evaluation criteria (scored 1-5):
Variant Familiar Scannable Effort Growth Scale Total
1. Current+Reorg4352317
2. Apple Settings5545423
3. Minimal2442315
4. Tabbed3423416
5. Hub & Spoke3313515
6. Search First3522517
7. Spotify3233213
8. Dense Grid1322210
9. Priority2424416
10. Single Card2242111
Why Variant 2 wins each category:
  • Familiarity (5/5) — Jakob's Law: users expect your app to work like every other iOS app. Apple Settings = the universal reference point. Zero learning curve.
  • Scannability (5/5) — Miller's Law compliant sections (≤4 items each). Hick's Law: faster decisions. Section titles match mental models ("Appearance" = how it looks).
  • Implementation effort (4/5) — Reuses 100% of existing components (SettingsRow, SettingsSection). Only 3 new small files. No new navigation patterns.
  • Growth potential (5/5) — Profile card with premium badge (conversion), Support section visibility (ratings + shares), Destructive actions separated (churn prevention).
  • Scalability (4/5) — Adding new settings = add a row to the right section. New sections slot in naturally. Could evolve to hub-and-spoke (V5) later if settings grow past ~25.

Why not the runners-up:
  • V1 (17pts) — Safe but doesn't solve the real UX problems. Just rearranging deck chairs.
  • V6 Search (17pts) — Great for 50+ settings. Overkill for 15. The effort-to-payoff ratio is bad.
  • V4 Tabbed (16pts) — Adds navigation complexity and hides content behind tabs. Users can't see everything at once.
  • V9 Priority (16pts) — Clever but needs analytics data you don't have yet. Ship V2 now, evolve to V9 later if data shows clear usage patterns.
