Next pass — before / after

Three remaining changes, drawn with the app's real tokens (#F5F1ED canvas, #F8F5F1 paper, Literata + DM Sans, shadows.cardLifted). Left is what ships today; right is the proposal.

1 · Habit Detail — white cards → paper

Detail already gets the typography right (serif title, serif month). But its cards are pure white while Settings, Analytics and the Habit Browser now sit on warm paper — so the screen reads colder than everything around it. One-line change now that getPaperSurface exists. The dashed NOTE box also becomes a recessed well, matching the danger-zone and START SMALL pattern.

2 · Edit Habit — the biggest outlier left

Centred sans title, three centred uppercase grey micro-labels, a stark white pill input, and free-floating controls with no card structure. It's the screen every user meets when creating their first habit, and it shares almost nothing with the rest of the app. The proposal: kicker + serif title like Settings, serif section headings, everything inside paper cards, emoji tiles as rounded squares with an ink selection, and cadence chips as pills. Colour dots stay saturated — that palette is the user's data, not chrome.

3 · Home habit names — sans → serif (the risky one)

HabitCard.styles.ts renders habit names in DM Sans; the Habit Browser renders the same habit in Literata. Same object, two typefaces, depending on which screen you're on. Serif closes that gap and makes the daily surface feel authored — but home is the surface you scan every morning, where density and legibility beat beauty. Density is unchanged here; only the typeface moves. Judge this one on scan-ability, not on looks.

My read: ship 1 and 2. Number 3 is genuinely arguable — serif costs a little scanning speed on long habit names ("30-Minute Brain-Boosting Cardio" is where it hurts most), and home is the one screen where that cost is paid daily. If you want the consistency without the cost, the alternative is going the other way: make the Browser card titles sans, and keep serif for headings only.
