UX
The green dot + bold green "March" in the header gives an immediate month anchor. The "MAR" prefix inside the first ring reinforces it at the strip level — two layers of month context, both lightweight. When this week doesn't start on the 1st (e.g. March 8–14), the first ring just shows "8" with no prefix — the "MAR" only appears on the actual 1st of the month, making it a genuine calendar landmark.
Premium Feel
The green dot + green month text creates a cohesive "active status" visual language across the header. The month label inside the ring is a detail that says "we thought about every cell" — the kind of microinteraction that makes users screenshot your app and share it. Premium apps earn trust through consistent, small details rather than big flashy features.

UX
This is where the combined approach really pays off. The "MAR" inside the Saturday ring instantly explains the 28 → 1 jump — no mental math needed. The green header says "March 2" (the latest date in the range, which is the most relevant), and the ring label handles the boundary clarification. Two complementary signals, zero confusion.
Premium Feel
Handling edge cases gracefully is the #1 signal of a well-engineered product. Most habit apps treat cross-month weeks as an afterthought. By showing the "MAR" label exactly at the transition point, users feel the app is spatially aware of their calendar — it understands time the way they do. This builds the "this app just gets it" feeling that drives word-of-mouth and retention.

UX
When swiping through past weeks, the bold green "January" is the first thing you see — unmissable temporal anchor. The "13 – 19" range is secondary detail. When you swipe past the month boundary, "January" snaps to "February" — a clear, satisfying signal that you've crossed months. No first-of-month ring label needed here since the week is mid-month, so the strip stays clean.
Premium Feel
The bold green month name while time-traveling makes reviewing past habits feel intentional and oriented rather than "scrolling through noise." It's the difference between a photo gallery with date headers and one without — the former feels curated, the latter feels like a dump. Users who review their history regularly (your most engaged, most likely-to-convert segment) will feel this the most.
