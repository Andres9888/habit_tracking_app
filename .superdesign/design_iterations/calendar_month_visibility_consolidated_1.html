UX
Drops "Thu" (redundant — the strip already shows day-of-week labels). Promotes "March" to 15px bold, making it the first thing the eye reads. Day number stays at 14px medium. Reduces the date string from 3 info-pieces to 2.
Premium Feel
Removing redundant info is a hallmark of premium apps (Things 3, Fantastical). Clutter-free UI signals "every pixel was considered" — users associate this with quality they'd pay for.

UX
"March" at 20px Literata is the visual anchor — your eye hits it first. "Thursday, 5th" at 13px is secondary context. The hierarchy mirrors how your brain organizes time: month first, then day. Swiping from March to February is impossible to miss when the big word changes.
Premium Feel
The Literata serif for the month name adds editorial warmth — it's the same typographic treatment Apple Calendar and Timepage use. Serif + sans-serif pairing signals design sophistication that free apps don't bother with. Users notice this even if they can't articulate it.

UX
Same single line as current, but font mixing creates instant hierarchy. "March" in 16px Literata bold reads as a label. "5th" in 13px light gray reads as a detail. Your eye separates them without conscious effort — it's ~200ms faster to find the month than the current flat string.
Premium Feel
Font mixing within a single line is a detail most apps skip — it requires intentional design. This is the kind of "I can't explain why, but this looks polished" detail that separates paid apps from free ones. The contrast ratio between the two weights communicates careful craft.

UX
The first day of the displayed week shows "MAR" above its number inside the ring. The last day shows "W1" (week 1). This puts month context directly where you're already looking — in the day strip — without changing the header at all. Especially useful on cross-month weeks where "FEB" and "MAR" would appear on their respective first days.
Premium Feel
Information-dense but not cluttered — this is the design principle behind luxury watch complications. Showing month context inside the ring makes every week feel like it has a "place" in the calendar. Free apps treat weeks as disposable; premium apps give each week an identity.

UX
Drops "Thu", adds "Wk 1" — same amount of text but different information. Week-of-month tells you where you are within the month (early, mid, late) which pure dates don't. The dot separator creates a clear visual break between the date and the week context. When swiping, "Wk 1" changing to "Wk 4" reinforces that you've moved far.
Premium Feel
Week numbering is a power-user feature found in Fantastical and Outlook — it signals that this app understands structured time management. Users who track habits weekly (your entire user base) benefit from this context. It makes the app feel like it was designed by someone who actually uses it.

UX
The green dot replaces the calendar emoji — it's a color anchor that your eye can find faster than an icon. "March" in the brand green at 15px bold ties it to the app's primary action color, making it feel like an interactive label (because it is — it opens the calendar). Drops "Thu" like V1.
Premium Feel
The dot + colored text creates a "status indicator" pattern used in Linear, Notion, and GitHub. It makes the month feel like a live, active element rather than a static label. This micro-interaction language (green = active) builds a visual system users learn to trust — and trust is what converts free users to paid.
All variations use the app's design system: DM Sans (body) + Literata (display), warm stone palette (#FAF8F5, #EDEAE5, #DDD8D2), forest green primary (#059669), burnished gold streak (#E8B94D), 44px progress rings.
