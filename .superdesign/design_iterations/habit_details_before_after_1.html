Design review · Habit Details prototype

Before / After — five improvement areas

The direction is right and already shipped into the app. These are the seams left in the prototype itself — 1, 2 and 4 are mechanical bugs that erode trust when someone pokes the live demo; 3 and 5 are design decisions worth settling before this screen is used as reference again. After-panels marked live are interactive — try them.

1

The strip and the calendar disagree on what day it is

Week strip is Monday-first with “today” on Saturday; the month grid is Sunday-first and July 12, 2026 — its “today” — is a Sunday. One screen, two week conventions, two different todays.

Strip: today = Saturday · Grid: today = Sunday the 12th

Two mental models of the same week, four centimetres apart. QA against a real calendar fails immediately.

After

July 2026

Mon-start
One convention (Mon-start) · today = Sunday the 12th in both

Monday-start keeps the strip’s story — six days of history behind you, today at the end. The grid pads two cells instead of three and the 12th lands in the Sunday column. Data-only fix.

2

Tabs promise navigation they can’t deliver · live

Max scroll never brings Goal or Strength near the top, so the offset-based spy snaps the indicator back to Calendar right after you tap. Fix: pick the section with the greatest visible area instead of comparing offsets.

3

Two progress metaphors race each other

The ring fills toward best; the Goal bar fills toward goal. The demo hides it because best == goal == 21. The moment they diverge, two fills answer “how far am I?” differently.

Same streak, 40% on the ring, 57% on the bar — which one am I?

Both fills are honest, but the user reads them as one progress metric drawn twice, inconsistently.

After · one hero, one line

Ring stays the only fill. The goal becomes a quiet checkpoint line.

The ring is the emotional metric — keep it the hero. The goal reads as a milestone on that path, not a rival meter. (Alternative: drive the ring off the goal instead — fine too, but pick one.)

4

Today shows “done” in the grid while the chip says Pending

Day 12 is pre-seeded into the done-set, so the calendar contradicts the status chip, and Mark-as-done doesn’t move the month count. Demo-data bug that undercuts the interaction the prototype exists to prove.

Marking done changes nothing visible in this section — the proof moment is dead on arrival.

Today starts hollow with the today-ring. Completing it flips chip, cell and count together — one source of truth, visible cause and effect.

5

The settled CTA is still a live toggle

Tapping “Done today” silently undoes the completion. This exact pattern has already bitten real users in the app (the accidental Run-habit untoggle). Undo belongs in the toast window, not on the celebration button.

Before · live — tap it twice
12day streak
Second tap — a fidget, a pocket touch — silently deletes the completion.

The most prominent, most-tapped control on the screen doubles as its own destructive inverse.

After · live — undo lives in the toast
12day streak
Logged · streak +1

Completion is one-way on the button. A second tap just re-offers Undo in the toast — intent must be explicit, accidents are free.

My read

The design itself doesn’t need rescuing — hierarchy, tone and the momentum loop are the best version of this screen you have, and the app already runs on them. Every issue above is a seam in the prototype’s execution, not its argument.

1, 2 and 4 are mechanical: wrong demo data and a spy heuristic that fails at this content height. Fix them and the prototype survives any live poke. 3 and 5 are actual decisions: one progress metaphor (I’d keep the ring and demote the goal to a checkpoint line) and one-way completion with undo in the toast — the app’s own history shows the toggle CTA causes real accidental undos.

Minor leftovers, no mock needed: the milestone slot reserves ~20px of dead space at non-milestone streaks (collapse it); strength “72 / 100” and the 78% rate are hardcoded in JSX instead of living in demoHabit.ts; the scroll handler re-queries the DOM per tick.
