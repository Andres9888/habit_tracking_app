Quantitative comparison

Dimension Variant A — Questionnaire Variant B — Benefit-driven
Total steps 17 16
Selection screens (require user input) 5 (Goal, Pain Points, Category, App Demo, paywall picker) 2 (Pick 3 Habits, paywall picker)
Time to complete (estimated) ~5–8 minutes ~3–5 minutes
Paywall pattern Hard — must subscribe to enter app Soft — free tier escape with 3-habit cap
Apple App Review risk Medium (hard paywall is allowed but scrutinized) Low
Trial-to-paid conversion (claimed) ~10–15% (Prayer Lock benchmark) ~5–10% at trial; +3–5% over 90 days from free
Net 90-day LTV (estimated) Higher per-paid-user, lower total Lower per-paid-user, higher total (free users + delayed conversions)
Strict alignment with Jordan Morgan video for habit trackers No — questionnaire is for fitness/calorie/sleep apps in his taxonomy Yes — habit tracker is the canonical benefit-driven example
Investment / commitment effect (Cialdini) Strong — 5 selection screens build sunk-cost Weaker — 2 selection screens, less invested
Reusable as paid-ad creatives Limited (questionnaire screens don't sell) High (4 benefit screens are standalone hooks)
How the test works once both variants ship:
  • 50/50 random assignment on first onboarding launch, persisted to AsyncStorage. Same user always sees the same variant.
  • Console-log analytics (visible in Metro/Xcode): variant assignment, step entries, completion, paywall action.
  • Real analytics integration (Mixpanel/PostHog/Sentry) is a separate workstream. Treat the v1 ship as a UX-comparison framework, not a true statistically-rigorous A/B until analytics land.
  • Dev override via env or AsyncStorage debug key for QA testing.
  • Rollback path: each commit independently revertable. If variant B converts catastrophically worse, kill the assignment hook and force everyone to A.
