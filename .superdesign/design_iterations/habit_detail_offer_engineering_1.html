One state line, one action with the smallest version pre-made, then the numbers. Only one CTA is ever visible in the real app — the sticky bar fades in exactly as the hero toggle scrolls out. Both are drawn here so the static mock shows the handoff.

The changes

Numbers match §4 of the review. Effort chips are the review’s own S / M / L. Every revenue line is a retention claim — ChainDay is single-tier with no feature gating, so the only money lever on this screen is keeping the user long enough to convert and renew.

0

Fix the home / detail today mismatch

TrustEffort S–M
WhyObserved live at 10:04 on Sep 2: the home strip said “Today · Sep 1” while Detail’s week strip marked Wed Sep 2 as today. Two today computations on different cadences, a known midnight-rollover gap.
HowSingle source of truth for the current date across the date hooks; re-derive on app foreground, not only on mount.
SolvesThe app contradicts itself about what day it is, which quietly voids every other date sentence on the page.
RetentionShip this before any copy work: changes 1, 4 and 8 all assert dates, and none of them are worth writing on top of a date the app gets wrong.
1

Recovery headline counts the actual misses

SupportEffort S
WhyStreak guilt is the #1 named churn trigger in habit apps — “streak resets to zero, red X, guilt spiral.” The card is the strongest fear-removal on the page, so it has to be exactly true: it said “Yesterday got away” under two dashed circles. And “Your 2-day record still stands” is not reassurance at n=2, it is a reminder of how little there is.
HowCount misses since the last completion in useHabitDetailDayState.ts; recoveryHeadlineCopy spells it (“Two days got away.”). Floor the record clause in recoveryBodyCopy at bestStreak < 3 → “Today starts the next one.”
SolvesThe sentence about my miss now matches what I can see, and it points forward instead of pricing my tiny record.
RetentionRecovery state is the leak: 43% of habit-app users quit inside 30 days and the broken streak is the top named trigger. Every user kept through a miss is a renewal kept.
2

Habit-aware smallest version, not the habit name pasted in

Done-for-youEffort S
Why“Try two minutes of 24-Hour Purchase Rule” is nonsense for a rule / abstinence / decision habit. twoMinuteHint knows nothing about habit type, while templates already carry startSmallVersion that nothing on this screen reads.
HowDetailHeroBanner.utils.ts: hint = template startSmallVersion when the habit came from a template; otherwise a type-neutral fallback (“Do the smallest version you’d still call done. It counts.”). Label becomes “Smallest version”.
SolvesThe pre-made tiny version is the whole done-for-you promise; a nonsense hint burns it and teaches me the app doesn’t know my habit.
RetentionThe smallest version is the cheapest way to convert a would-be miss into a check-in — and check-ins on miss-days are exactly the days that decide whether the trial ends in a subscription.
3

Analytics door counts down instead of promising a dead end

SystemsEffort S
Why“See what helps you stay consistent” promises support that does not exist until MIN_DAYS_OF_DATA = 14 plus per-insight thresholds. For a new habit the door opens onto a page with no verdict and no rows — a dead end dressed as a promise.
HowRecordDoors.tsx: while locked, subtitle = “Unlocks after 14 days of check-ins · N to go” with a hairline progress rail; swap to the current copy once MIN_DAYS_OF_DATA is met.
SolvesI stop tapping into an empty room, and the wait becomes visible progress instead of a broken promise.
RetentionTurns the app’s best support surface into a reason to reach day 14 — the same window where most churn happens. Progressive disclosure also cuts task time 20–40% (NN/g).
4

“0 days logged” → “5 days left”

SupportEffort S
WhyA zero above two dashed misses is the red-X pattern in softer clothes, and it is the second grade on the page before the user has done anything. The dots are already gentle — the count is the problem.
HowThisWeekCard/WeekCardHeader.tsx: when doneCount === 0 and scheduled days remain, show “N days left” (or nothing). Keep “N days logged” the moment the number means something.
SolvesThe week reads as runway, not as a scorecard I am already losing.
RetentionRemoves one of the three grades the recovery user meets before any instruction. Fewer verdicts on the miss-day screen is the cheapest churn-trigger removal available.
5

Strength gets its unit and a human caption

SystemsEffort S
WhyThe dial shows a bare “8” with no visible unit; the word “Strength” only exists in the a11y label, so the level name does the labelling the number should. The default caption describes the algorithm, not the person.
HowStrengthSnapshot.tsx: “STRENGTH” overline above the level name and “/100” under the dial value. Default caption → “Grows with every check-in. A miss dips it, never resets it.” Recovery caption stays “Dipped, not reset. Recent days still count most.”
SolvesI can tell what 8 is a number of, and the sentence talks to me instead of documenting the decay model.
RetentionStrength is the only number that survives a miss. Making it legible on the miss-day is what stops “my streak is gone” from meaning “I have nothing left here”.
6

The why line stays visible in recovery

SupportEffort S
WhyresolveWhy output renders only in the neutral ready state — so the why is hidden in recovery and after completion, the two moments it is most useful. The reason I started is the strongest counterweight to a miss.
HowDetailHeroBanner.tsx: compact HeroWhyPill variant — one italic serif line under the recovery card and the completed card. One line, truncated, never a block.
SolvesOn the day I want to quit, the page reminds me what I was buying with this habit.
RetentionReconnects the miss-day to the outcome the subscription is actually for. Cheapest possible re-motivation: data the user already typed, shown at the one moment it pays.
7

If-then plan under the title, not buried in Edit

SystemsEffort M
WhyThe line prints “Daily” while the habit already stores reminderTime, cueAfterBehavior, cueTime and cueLocation — none of which reach the screen. Implementation intentions run d = 0.65 across 94 studies; the contingent “after X, I do Y” format is the effect. Making the user remember their own plan is the exact thinking cost the framework says to remove.
HowHeroTitleRow.tsx + scheduleLabel: render “After morning coffee · 7:30 reminder” when cue/reminder data exists; fall back to today’s “Daily”. Tap opens Edit (pencil glyph signals it).
SolvesThe plan is on the page at the moment of action, so I don’t have to reconstruct when and after what.
RetentionThe largest documented effect size available on this screen, using data already in the record. Better follow-through is more check-ins, and check-in frequency is the renewal predictor.
8

Goal arrives already set — ladder, not picker

Done-for-youEffort M
WhyAt streak 0, in recovery, strength 8, the card asks the user to choose a commitment and re-states the record the recovery card just named. Thinking + fear + a duplicate number on one screen — and “Give this run a finish line” when there is no run.
HowApply suggestedGoal(bestStreak) at habit creation / first open and render GoalSetCard immediately with a quiet “Change”. Ladder marks come straight from buildLadder(0, 2, 7) → today · 2★ · 7; plan sentence from goalPlanSentence. Adjust sheet remains the only place to remove or customise.
SolvesThe default is already chosen and stated in dates — a plan I can act on, not a decision I have to make on a bad day.
RetentionA set goal gives the run a visible finish line with two dated milestones inside the coming week. Near-term, dated targets are what pull a user back tomorrow rather than “sometime”.
9

Sticky bottom CTA once the hero toggle scrolls off

Done-for-youEffort M
WhyThe only action on the page sits in the top third — the stretch zone — and disappears after any scroll. Thumb-zone data: 96% tap accuracy in the bottom third vs 61% in the stretch zone, and bottom CTAs are ~2.7× faster. A sticky bar shipped once before and was removed.
HowHabitDetailContent.tsx: same toggle component, same state, same undo, pinned above a soft cream gradient; it fades in only when the hero toggle leaves the viewport, so there is never a second affordance on screen at once.
SolvesWherever I am on the page, the one thing I came to do is under my thumb.
RetentionShortest path from opening the screen to logging the day. This is the metric to instrument first — detail_open → complete_today in recovery state.
10

Rest-day mark — a declared skip that isn’t a miss

SupportEffort L
WhyEvery scheduled day is complete or missed; there is no “not today”. The strength model already absorbs misses, but the calendar still draws a dashed miss on a planned rest day. Duolingo’s streak freeze cut churn 21% among at-risk users, and separating the streak from the daily goal lifted D14 retention 3.3%.
HowDay screen + tracking: a rest-day mark needs either a tracking kind or a separate table, plus rules for how it reads on the week strip and in Analytics. Product decision first, then data.
SolvesA day I deliberately took off stops being recorded as a failure I have to recover from.
RetentionThe one change here with an external retention number attached — but it is a schema and product commitment, so it belongs after 1–9 have proved the copy layer.

What stays

These already do the job the framework asks for. Nothing below is on the table.

  • The toggle + undo on one control. The 20%: one tap, both states, no second affordance. Best element on the page — only its position moves.
  • Amber wash, never red. A miss is a state, not a verdict. The whole page leans on this and it is already right.
  • Strength survives a miss. Proportional decay, never zero — the only number that keeps the user attached after a break.
  • Verdict-first Analytics. “Covering Fridays would have put August at 93%” is the best support element in the app; only its door subtitle changes.
  • Day & History screens. Honest, correctable, one interactive month, a stepper between days. No change.
  • InsightLine hidden below 14 days. Correct progressive disclosure — one grounded sentence with evidence behind it, or nothing.
