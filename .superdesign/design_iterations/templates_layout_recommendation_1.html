Why this layout is stronger

The current screen is visually solid, but it still makes users decide too much too early. This proposal improves the hierarchy by reducing scanning cost, improving information scent, and using progressive disclosure more consistently.

1 Search plus chips above the fold

Users should not have to remember the right keyword or scroll to the category grid just to narrow the library. Putting quick chips under search supports recognition over recall and reduces decision time.

2 One hero, one clear focal point

The hero becomes the dominant entry point for users who want guidance, instead of competing equally with every other section. That creates a clearer visual hierarchy and a better first scan.

3 Trending remains the low-friction action zone

Trending cards stay small and quick because their job is speed, not depth. They support a one-tap import path and keep momentum for users who already know they want something popular.

4 Packs come before deep browsing

Pack import is a higher-value action than broad category exploration. Showing packs before the full grid surfaces the most leverage earlier without removing the exploratory path.

5 Category grid becomes optional exploration

Categories are still useful, but they should not be the only category path. Keeping them lower on the page makes sense once chips and curated sections already cover fast discovery.

6 Result cards stop doing preview work

The list cards are intentionally compact. Long science boxes and full explanations belong in the preview modal. That separation improves scanning speed and respects progressive disclosure on small screens.

Key behavior changes shown here

  • Use one term consistently: Templates, not a mix of library and habits.
  • Search feedback explains why results matched instead of showing a generic title.
  • Result cards show compact metadata only; rich science detail moves to preview.
  • Quick filters reduce the need to scroll to category discovery.
