→ Why it's better
⧗ Zeigarnik Effect: The nearly-full bar with a glowing edge is viscerally incomplete. That tiny gap screams "finish me" — a single unfilled 6px dot can't create this tension.
📊 NNGroup: At 83% fill, the bar signals "almost there" instantly. With dots, 5/6 vs 4/6 filled looks nearly identical — the bar makes the gap dramatic.

The pulsing glow at the bar's edge draws the eye to what's missing. Users report feeling "pulled" to close the gap — the same mechanic that makes progress bars addictive.

→ Why it's better
⧗ Loss Aversion (Kahneman): The full-width pulsing red bar is a visual wound — impossible to ignore. Six tiny pulsing dots create urgency at 6×6px total; the bar creates it across the entire width.
🎯 Fitts's Law: The bar is a tappable target (scrolls to first habit). At full-width × 5px, it's ~15× the area of the dots. In a panic, larger targets = faster action.

The pulsing empty bar combined with the red today-circle creates a visual "emergency" without any alarm copy. The UI communicates urgency through color and motion alone.

→ Why it's better
🏔 Peak-End Rule: Users judge experiences by their peak moment and how they end. A full-width golden shimmering bar is a dramatically better peak than 6 golden dots — it's the visual equivalent of a victory lap.

Screenshot-worthy. This golden bar is the most visually distinct element across all states. It's the exact moment users share on social media — every pixel of celebration earns organic growth.

→ Why it's better
👁 Z-Pattern: Identical layout regardless of state. Users build spatial memory — "top-left = how I'm doing, bar = my progress, bottom = navigation." No re-scanning needed across sessions.
🎯 Fitts's Law: The empty bar is the primary CTA for new/returning users. It's the widest tappable element on screen — effortless to engage with, even one-handed.

Warm, not shaming. The empty bar is neutral gray (not red). The ☀️ emoji adds friendliness. This is an open lane waiting to be filled — inviting, not demanding.
