Design review · Offer Engineering

The app already is the offer. Users just never see it.

Framework: people buy pain-removal (80%) over promised results (20%), and pay for speed and ease — via Done-For-You, Process, and Support. The codebase scores 3/3 pillars. The live experience delivers roughly 1/3, because the highest-value surfaces are flag-disabled or inert. Two mock pairs below: first touch, and the Habit Detail page during days 0–14.

Funnel off16-step onboarding + trial paywall fully built, disabled in featureFlags.ts. New user lands on an empty list. Revenue = $0 by construction.
Dead first 14 daysDetail-page insights gate on 14 days of data — placeholder shown during the exact window most users churn.
Fake escape hatch“Only have 2 minutes?” logs nothing. The one feature aimed at hard days doesn’t count the rep.

  • 1Before: the 2-minute link is framing only — HeroTwoMinute.tsx logs nothing. User taps it on their worst day and the app shrugs. That is the moment the framework says to remove pain, and we add it.
  • 2Before: the “Noticing” section — the page’s core value — is a dashed placeholder for the exact 14 days when churn peaks. The page is weakest when the user is weakest.
  • 3Before: one habit-level note. Users expect a per-day journal tied to each check-in; a single running blob discourages writing anything.
  • 4After: 2-minute version is a real logging action (counts as completion, flagged as minimal). Protects the streak, keeps the identity vote.Support
  • 5After: provisional insight sourced from the habit’s own template science (already curated in scienceEnrichment/*.data.ts) fills days 0–14, with an honest “your data takes over” progress line.ProcessDFY
  • 6After: per-day note attached to the check-in, promoted into the card the user already touches daily.Support
  • 7After: single, quiet premium touchpoint — streak shield on the milestone hook. Highest-emotion surface finally has one monetization moment; deliberately not on the recovery card (never sell against shame).
