Why Each Change Exists — Triple Value Filter
Change Pain It Solves Value to User Value to Business Metric
Amber "Explore" pill Templates pill looks identical to Settings — users don't notice it. Discovery of new habits is buried behind a ghost button. Warm amber creates visual hierarchy: green = create, amber = discover, neutral = configure. Users instantly know where to find new habits. Templates = premium content funnel. Amber draws the eye without being aggressive. "Explore" label creates curiosity vs clinical "Templates". Template CTR
Progress ring + counter User has to mentally count checkmarks to know "how many left?" — cognitive load on the most repeated screen. Glanceable progress: "1 of 3" with a visual ring. Completion proximity effect — when ring is 2/3 full, motivation spikes. Progress visibility drives daily completion rate. Higher completion = longer streaks = higher retention = higher LTV. Completion %
All-done celebration After completing all habits, nothing happens. The most satisfying moment in the app has zero reward signal. FAB morphs + → ✓, ring fills green, "All done!" label. Variable reward in the habit loop (Nir Eyal's Hook model). Dopamine hit at completion → user returns tomorrow → compounds into streaks → streak protection becomes a premium upsell moment. Day 7 Retention
Settings de-emphasis Settings and Templates have identical visual weight, but Settings is used 1x/month vs Templates potentially daily. Reduces visual noise. Settings is findable when needed but doesn't compete for attention with the action buttons. Attention budget is finite. Every pixel of attention on Settings is stolen from Add and Explore — the two revenue drivers. Focus Score
Rejected — Failed the Triple Filter
Floating island bar (detached from edges)
Why not: Breaks the app's established edge-to-edge frosted glass pattern. Adds 16px padding on each side = narrower touch targets. Looks modern but doesn't solve any user pain — it's decoration. Fails the pain filter.
Full progress bar below the bar
Why not: Competes with CalendarTimeline's completion ring that already shows daily progress. Two progress indicators = confusion about source of truth. The compact ring + counter around the FAB delivers the same info in 0 extra vertical space. Fails the value filter (redundant).
Confetti particle burst on all-done
Why not: App's motion philosophy is "no decorative loops." Confetti is fun for 3 days then annoying for 300. The ring-fill + icon morph is the right level of reward — satisfying but not patronizing. Fails the retention filter (novelty wears off).
Red notification badge on Templates
Why not: Red = alarm, urgency, error. A habit app should feel calm, not anxiety-inducing. The amber warmth draws the eye without manufactured urgency. Fails the UX filter (wrong emotional register).
