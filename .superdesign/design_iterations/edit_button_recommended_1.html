Recommendation

Whole-row tappable, with "Tap to edit ›" hint

One affordance for every Advanced Option. No badges, no tiers, no upsell. The cleanest version of itself.

Discoverability

The whole row is the button. Users don't have to spot a tiny green tag — the value, the label, the hint, and the chevron all read as one tappable surface. First-time users see "Tap to edit ›" and immediately understand the options are customizable. That moment of perceived depth in the first 30 seconds is what drives the decision to keep going.

Friction = the biggest hit target possible

~360px wide × 64px tall per row. Compared to the current ~50×24px pill, that's roughly an 18× larger tap target. Fewer mis-taps, less micro-aiming, especially one-handed on the go. Friction in habit creation is one of the strongest predictors of drop-off — every extra second of "wait, is this clickable?" costs you.

Conversion lever

Depth visible = product feels more valuable. Users who interact with at least one Advanced Option during onboarding convert at materially higher rates in habit/productivity benchmarks. Making customization obvious — not buried behind an "EDIT" tag — is the cheapest way to surface that depth without adding features.

Retention lever

Customized habits stick. When a user tailors strength curve, growth icons, and streak goal to themselves, the habit feels theirs — psychological ownership compounds into return visits. The whole-row affordance maximizes the chance that customization actually happens, which directly lowers churn.

Hierarchy preserved

Nothing competes with Save. The row affordance is subtle gray-on-near-white at rest, going green only on touch. The dark-green "Create Habit" CTA at the bottom remains the loudest thing on the page. The Edit affordances support the form; they don't fight it.

Why not the alternatives

B (solid green CTA on every row) — three loud buttons stacked would compete with Save and feel pushy.
F (gradient pill) — its visual punch was earning its keep only as a premium-tier upsell signal. With no Pro tier, it's just noise.
E (icon-only pencil) — too subtle, the same problem as today.
A (bigger pill) — incremental; doesn't fix the "this looks like a label" issue.
C (outline button) — clean, but still asks the user to aim at a small target. D dominates it on hit-area.
