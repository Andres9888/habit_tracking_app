Serial Position Effect

Streak only in hero badge (primacy). 3 stat chips show unique complementary data — no duplication.

Zeigarnik Effect

Calendar moved up — visible gaps (missed days) create psychological tension that drives users to act. Most actionable element is now easy to reach.

Cognitive Load

Simple → Complex progression. Calendar (scannable grid) before Strength (dense analytics) prevents the "data wall" feeling.

Peak-End Rule

Strength chart shows upward growth trajectory — users end the page on a positive, reflective note. They remember their improvement, not just data.

Why This Order — The Science

🧠

Serial Position Effect

Murdock, 1962

Users remember the first and last items best. Streak badge (first) and growth chart (last) are the two most motivating elements — placed at the two positions with highest recall.

⚡

Zeigarnik Effect

Zeigarnik, 1927

Incomplete tasks stay top-of-mind. The calendar's visible gaps (missed days) create productive tension. Moving it up means users encounter this "must complete" signal before deep analytics.

📊

Cognitive Load Theory

Sweller, 1988

Working memory handles ~4 chunks. Current order dumps 4 stat chips → ring → chart → 3-column stats consecutively. New order: simple grid first, then progressively denser analytics — simple → complex.

🎯

Peak-End Rule

Kahneman, 1993

People judge experiences by the peak moment and how they end. The strength chart with its upward curve is a positive "you're growing" narrative — the perfect emotional ending that users carry with them.
