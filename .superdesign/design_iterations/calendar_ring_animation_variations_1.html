Why it works
Overshoot principle (Disney's 12 Principles): Scale past target then settle creates organic, living feel. Issue: The 1.2x overshoot can feel "jumpy" on repeated views and draws disproportionate attention to the animation itself rather than the data it represents. Overshoot works best as a one-time celebration, not a repeating entrance.

Science & UX Rationale
Miller's cognitive load theory: No overshoot = less visual processing per element. The 0.7→1 scale is sub-threshold for "motion" perception (Weber-Fechner law) — users perceive elements "appearing" rather than "bouncing." Material Design 3 guidance: Entrance animations should be "quick and subtle" to avoid competing with content. Best for: apps where the data (completion status) matters more than the animation.

Science & UX Rationale
Gravity metaphor (Gibson's affordance theory): Objects dropping from above match real-world physics — our visual system expects gravity. The slight bounce at landing (2px overshoot) signals "placed and settled." Spatial hierarchy: Downward motion implies the rings are being "delivered" from the header/month label area, reinforcing the calendar → day relationship. Works well with stagger to create a "rain" of rings settling into place.

Science & UX Rationale
Shape congruence (Gestalt): A circular reveal on a circular element creates formal harmony — the animation echoes the element's shape. Attentional spotlight theory (Posner): The expanding circle mirrors how visual attention radiates outward from a fixation point. Caveat: clip-path isn't available in React Native (would need SVG masking or Skia). This is the most "premium" feeling option but has the highest implementation complexity.

Science & UX Rationale
Binocular depth cues: Y-axis rotation implies the ring is "turning to face you" from another plane — creates a sense of 3D space in a 2D UI. Von Restorff effect (novelty): The 3D flip is uncommon in habit apps, making it memorable and differentiating. Risk: Can feel gimmicky if overused. The 3D perspective transform works in Reanimated but is more expensive than 2D transforms. Best suited for a single "completion" celebration rather than all 7 rings at once.

Science & UX Rationale
Disney's squash & stretch: The double oscillation (1.15 → 0.92 → 1.05 → 0.98 → 1) mimics elastic material settling. Each bounce is smaller, creating a natural damping curve. Hedonic adaptation resistance: The multi-phase settle is harder for the brain to "tune out" compared to a single bounce — it stays engaging longer across repeated sessions. Tradeoff: At 650ms, it's the longest animation. On rapid week-swiping, this could feel laggy. Would need to cancel/skip on gesture start.

Science & UX Rationale
Gestalt continuity: Upward motion aligns with the scroll direction (content rises into view from below the fold). The brain expects this direction when new content appears in a vertically-scrolling app. Apple HIG "fluid" transitions: iOS system animations use rise-from-below for non-modal content (notifications, list items). Users are pre-trained to interpret this as "new content arriving." At 350ms with no overshoot, this is the most performant and least intrusive option — it communicates "here's your week" without demanding attention. Best for a utility the user sees 5-10x daily.

Science & UX Rationale
Size-weight illusion: Starting large (1.6x) and compressing to 1x creates a perceived "weight" — the rings feel substantial, like physical objects being placed. Authority signaling: The stamp motion communicates decisiveness ("here's your data, it's definitive"). Contrast with V0: Where V0 starts small and overshoots up, V7 starts large and settles down. V7 feels more "confident" while V0 feels more "excited." At 350ms, it's fast and direct.

Science & UX Rationale
Object permanence (Piaget): The ring "was always there" as a dot — it just grew to its full size. This creates narrative continuity (the data existed, now it's revealed) vs. V0 where the ring materializes from nothing. Biomimicry: Growth from a seed-point mimics organic expansion (cell division, plant growth). This resonates with the habit-building metaphor of "growing" consistency. Connects to the green dot header: If the header has a 6px green dot, the rings could feel like they're "born" from similar dots.

Science & UX Rationale
Sequential attention (Treisman's feature integration): The left-to-right wave matches reading direction and guides the eye through Mon→Sun, creating an implicit "story of your week." Each ring arriving one-by-one lets the brain register each day's status individually rather than as a blob. Satisficing cascade: The wave creates a micro-narrative where completed days "check in" one by one — this leverages the Zeigarnik effect (incomplete sequences are attention-holding). Implementation: This is purely a stagger delay on any base animation. Can combine with V1, V6, or V8 for different flavors.

Science & UX Rationale
Luminance transients (Yantis & Jonides): The opacity throb (fade in → slightly brighten → settle) creates a luminance change that reliably captures bottom-up attention without spatial displacement. The brain registers "something changed here" without tracking motion. Heart-rate metaphor: The single pulse echoes a heartbeat — subtle but alive. For a health/habit app, this biometric echo reinforces the "living system" metaphor. Lowest cognitive cost: No spatial displacement means the user's spatial map isn't disrupted. The rings appear "in place" and pulse once to say "I'm here."
