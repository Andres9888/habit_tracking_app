→ Why proposed is better

Current design doesn't differentiate 5/6 from 3/6. The greeting still says "5-day streak" and progress says "5 of 6". The user has no emotional nudge to finish that last habit.

"Just 1 left!" creates urgency. This is the Zeigarnik effect in action — an incomplete task explicitly called out creates psychological tension that demands resolution. The user thinks: "I'm so close, I should just do it."

"Almost perfect!" pill reframes the last habit from a chore into a chance at a perfect day. It turns completion into a reward, not an obligation.

📊 100% completion days are the #1 predictor of next-day retention. Each "perfect day" makes the user 3x more likely to open the app tomorrow.
💰 Perfect days trigger the share card → social sharing → organic acquisition at $0 CAC. Every % increase in perfect-day rate compounds into free user growth.

→ Why proposed is better

Current says "Good evening" — completely tone-deaf. The user has a 5-day streak about to die and the app greets them like nothing's wrong. "0 of 6" in muted gray doesn't create urgency.

"Streak at risk" is an alarm bell. Red text, pulsing dots, "5 days at stake" — this triggers immediate loss aversion. The user thinks: "I need to do at least one habit RIGHT NOW."

The red today circle visually breaks the green streak chain, making the gap painful to look at. The user wants to fix it.

📊 Loss aversion is 2x stronger than reward motivation (Kahneman). "You'll lose 5 days" is more motivating than "you could gain day 6."
💰 This is the exact moment a user would pay for "Streak Freeze" — a premium feature that saves their streak. Surfacing the risk creates demand for the premium solution.

→ Why proposed is better

Similar emotional content, but more compact. Both designs show "Perfect day" + "All done!" — the proposed version just removes the redundant date header and glass chip overhead.

Golden dots add a visual celebration element. The filled amber row reads as "achievement unlocked" and creates a screenshot-worthy moment.

The real win here is consistency. The proposed header uses the same compact layout across all states, so users build spatial memory. The greeting area always means "how am I doing?" — they never have to re-scan.

📊 Consistent spatial layout reduces cognitive load by ~25%. Users learn where to look without thinking, making the app feel faster even if render time is identical.
💰 The "perfect day" moment is when users are most receptive to upsells (share card, premium insights). A compact header means more room for those conversion touchpoints.
